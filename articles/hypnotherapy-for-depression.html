Hypnotherapy for Depression: What the Evidence Says

Key Takeaways

  • Depression is a medical condition. Hypnotherapy is not a replacement for your GP, medication, or the talking therapies recommended in NICE guidance.
  • The research on hypnosis for depression is smaller than the research on CBT. Some studies of cognitive hypnotherapy are encouraging, but larger independent trials are still needed.
  • Depression runs on rumination, hopeless predictions, and withdrawal. Those are patterns, and patterns are what hypnotherapy works on.
  • It suits people who are already under care and want an additional route in, or whose low mood has outlasted insight and effort.
  • If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, speak to a doctor or call emergency services first. Hypnotherapy is not the right starting point.

If you are reading this with a flat, heavy mood that will not lift, you have probably already tried the obvious things. You know you should exercise, sleep, and see people. Knowing has not been the problem.

I want to be straightforward about this subject. Depression is a serious condition and hypnotherapy is not a cure for it. What I can offer is an honest account of where the evidence stands, what cognitive hypnotherapy actually does, and where it fits alongside proper medical care.

What Depression Is, and What It Is Not

Depression is more than sadness. It is a cluster of changes in mood, energy, sleep, appetite, concentration, and the ability to feel pleasure. It tends to arrive with a story attached: that you are failing, that nothing will improve, that effort is pointless.

That story feels like an accurate reading of reality, which is exactly why it is so hard to argue with. It is better understood as a symptom. A depressed mind forecasts the future badly and remembers the past selectively. Your conscious reasoning is working with distorted inputs.

Depression is also not a character flaw or a failure of willpower. If you have a diagnosis, or suspect you have one, the first step is a conversation with your GP or a qualified clinician. Everything in this article assumes that conversation has happened or is happening.

Where the Evidence Stands

NICE guidance on depression in adults (NG222) recommends options such as CBT, behavioural activation, and medication depending on severity. Hypnotherapy is not on that list, and I would be misleading you if I said otherwise.

There is a body of smaller research on hypnosis as an addition to therapy. One often-cited randomised study by Alladin and Alibhai (2007) assigned 84 adults with depression to 16 weeks of either CBT alone or cognitive hypnotherapy, which adds hypnosis to a CBT framework. Both groups improved, and the hypnotherapy group showed larger reductions on measures of depression, anxiety, and hopelessness that were still present at follow-up. The study was led by the originator of the approach, and a later German trial found hypnotherapy was non-inferior to CBT rather than superior, so this is encouraging rather than conclusive.

My position is simple. The evidence supports treating hypnotherapy as a possible complement to established care, not an alternative to it. Anyone who tells you it replaces medication or therapy is overreaching.

The Patterns That Keep Low Mood Locked In

Most persistent low mood is maintained by a handful of loops. Rumination replays the same failures and worries until they feel like facts. Hopeless forecasting predicts that things will go badly, so you do not try. Withdrawal removes the very activities that used to give a lift, which confirms the prediction.

There is also the loss of reward. Many people with depression describe being able to do things but not feel anything from them. That makes the effort-to-payoff ratio look terrible, so motivation collapses.

None of these loops is solved by being told to think positively. They are learned habits of attention and prediction, running mostly below awareness. That is the level at which hypnosis operates.

If your mood has stayed low despite understanding exactly why, a different kind of approach may be worth a conversation.

What Cognitive Hypnotherapy Actually Does

The first session is a detailed conversation. I want to understand your history, what has and has not helped, what your GP knows, and what your days actually look like. If you are not already under medical care, I will ask you to arrange it before we go further.

In the hypnotic work itself, we use focused attention to do three things. We interrupt rumination by giving the mind a different, structured task. We rehearse small, specific actions in imagination so that they feel less heavy when you meet them in real life. And we work on the language of your self-talk, so that the harshest, most absolute statements loosen.

You also leave with a personalised recording and small practical tasks between sessions. In my experience this combination of rehearsal and real-world action matters more than the hypnotic state itself. It is the same principle as behavioural activation, delivered through a different door.

Important: If you are having thoughts of ending your life or harming yourself, please contact your GP, your local emergency number, or a crisis line such as Samaritans (116 123 in the UK and Ireland) before anything else. Hypnotherapy is not appropriate as a first response to a crisis.

Signs It May Be Time to Get Help

Low mood that lasts most of the day for two weeks or more, loss of interest in things you normally enjoy, changes in sleep or appetite, difficulty concentrating, and a persistent sense of worthlessness are all reasons to speak to your GP. They are not things to push through.

High-functioning people often delay. You can still be producing good work while privately struggling. Being able to function is not the same as being well, and getting support earlier tends to make recovery easier.

What I Will Ask You to Do Between Sessions

Depression shrinks your world, so the work between sessions matters. I will ask you to listen to your recording, and to take one or two small, specific actions each week that we agree in advance. These might be a short walk, a message to a friend, or a task you have been avoiding.

This is not about forcing positivity. It is about giving the nervous system evidence that effort can produce a small reward again. Hypnotic rehearsal makes the action feel lighter, and the action makes the new pattern real.

Who It Suits, and Who It Does Not

It tends to suit people who are already engaged with their GP or a therapist and want an additional approach. It also suits those whose understanding of their low mood is excellent but has not translated into change, which is a very common profile among analytical, high-performing people.

It is not suited to anyone in acute crisis, anyone experiencing psychosis or mania, or anyone who wants to stop prescribed medication. Changes to medication belong with your prescriber. I work alongside medication and never against it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can hypnotherapy cure depression?

No. Depression is a medical condition and hypnotherapy is not a cure. Some studies suggest cognitive hypnotherapy may help as an addition to established treatment, but it should not replace your GP, medication, or evidence-based talking therapy.

Can I have hypnotherapy while taking antidepressants?

Generally yes. Hypnotherapy works alongside medication and does not interact with it. Any change to your medication should be agreed with the person who prescribed it, never decided in a hypnotherapy session.

How many sessions are needed?

It varies, but most people working on low mood need several sessions rather than one. We review progress as we go and I will tell you honestly if it is not helping.

Is online hypnotherapy suitable for depression?

For many people, yes. Sessions are conducted by video, and the work is the same. The exception is anyone in crisis or at risk, who needs in-person or emergency support first.

Could hypnosis make my low mood worse?

Hypnosis itself is not harmful, but poorly structured work on distressing material can be unsettling. That is why a clear history, a structured plan, and realistic expectations matter. See our article on whether hypnotherapy is safe.
